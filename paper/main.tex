\begin{abstract}
Arbitrary-scale super-resolution exhibits shared frequency recovery, sustained responses to cell size, and gains from cell-conditioned learning. First, tested decoder families share recovery profiles, while stronger encoders, larger MLPs and longer training help mainly above input-normalised frequency $u\approx0.75$: longer training changes recovery below this boundary by at most 0.0013. Low-frequency saturation persists under Gaussian blur. Second, at equal decoder-input steps, cell-induced output changes generally remain as large or grow with scale: $J_6/J_2\ge1$ in 11 of 12 conditions, with point estimates 0.94--2.47. This descriptive pattern separates sustained output response from declining fixed-multiplier cost, which combines shrinking input steps and error alignment. Third, correctly paired cell improves learning: native cell exceeds constant-cell and shuffled-cell training by 0.100 and 0.097\,dB, respectively, under a matched 200-epoch, two-seed recipe. Both dimension-preserving controls are equivalent to no-cell within the registered $\pm0.03$\,dB margin. The gain therefore depends on correctly paired scale information, beyond retaining two extra inputs. Together, the findings distinguish where capacity helps, how cell changes the output, and why its pairing matters during learning.
\end{abstract}
\begin{keywords}
Arbitrary-scale super-resolution, implicit representation, frequency recovery, scale conditioning
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Continuous-coordinate super-resolution decodes local image features at arbitrary output locations. LIIF~\cite{chen2021liif} conditions this decoder on target pixel size, or \emph{cell}; subsequent designs use local Fourier estimators~\cite{lee2022lte}, integrated positional encoding~\cite{liu2021ipe}, implicit attention~\cite{cao2023ciaosr}, neural operators~\cite{wei2023srno}, Gaussian splatting~\cite{hu2024gaussiansr} or heat-field filtering~\cite{becker2025thera}. Which parts of the recovered signal benefit from these choices, and what information does cell contribute?

Prior work links performance to model size and training~\cite{nasir2026empirical} and studies spectral decoding's efficiency--accuracy trade-off~\cite{shad2026gblsr}. HyRA analyses nonlinear compensation of low-pass phase distortion~\cite{fsds2024}; LIIF, integrated encoding and heat fields connect cell to edges and filtering~\cite{chen2021liif,liu2021ipe,becker2025thera}. We locate capacity gains and distinguish cell-induced output change, error cost and training benefit.

Our findings are: (1) decoder families share recovery profiles, with capacity gains mainly above $u\approx0.75$ and lower-band changes no larger than 0.0013 from longer training; (2) equal-step cell response is sustained or larger at $\times6$ than $\times2$ in 11/12 conditions, separating response strength from error cost; and (3) correctly paired cell provides a 0.100/0.097\,dB advantage over constant/shuffled cell, both equivalent to no-cell within $\pm0.03$\,dB. Table~\ref{tab:gates} retains all prediction failures.

\section{Shared recovery profiles and capacity}
\label{sec:u}
\textbf{Models and measurement.} LIIF, LTE and SC-INR (an LTE-derived experimental variant with analytic sinc cell weights) use the EDSR-baseline architecture~\cite{lim2017edsr}, separately learned encoder weights and a common DIV2K~\cite{agustsson2017div2k} recipe: scales from $\mathcal U(1,4)$, 1000 epochs and three seeds. GaussianSR uses an external 2000-epoch EDSR checkpoint and provides an additional comparison. Matched 200-epoch LIIF arms change the encoder to RDN~\cite{zhang2018rdn} or SwinIR~\cite{liang2021swinir}, reduce the MLP from $4\times256$ to $2\times64$, or remove cell. These comparisons match recipes; parameter counts and compute differ.

Let $r=\max(|f_x|,|f_y|)$ be frequency in cycles per output pixel and $u=2sr$ its value relative to the input Nyquist limit at scale $s$. Recovery in band $b$ is $R_b=1-V_b/E_b$, where error energy $V_b$ and target energy $E_b$ are pooled over images before taking the ratio. Original crop analyses use DIV2K 0869--0900, $240^2$ centre crops, quarter-$u$ bins, anti-aliased bicubic inputs and a four-pixel shave. Full-image recovery additionally covers Set14~\cite{zeyde2010single}, BSD100~\cite{martin2001database} and Urban100~\cite{huang2015urban}. We evaluate $\times2/3/4/6$; $\times6$ lies outside the training scale range.

\textbf{A shared recovery regime.} Across $\times2/3/4$, the three matched decoder families have a maximum scale-specific band spread of 0.010, attained at $\times4$, in the registered $0.5\le u<2$ comparison (paired image-bootstrap 95\% interval $[0.007,0.016]$ at that scale). With GaussianSR included, all 54 full-image dataset--scale--band comparisons stay below 0.019. Closely shared profiles extend across the tested decoder forms and image collections (Fig.~\ref{fig:rec}a,b), although the stricter cross-scale-collapse prediction fails its registered tolerance gates.

\textbf{Capacity improves the incompletely recovered bands.} Longer training, stronger encoders and a larger MLP change recovery primarily from $u\approx0.75$ upward (Fig.~\ref{fig:rec}c). From epoch 100 to 1000, gains in $1\le u<2$ range from 0.0245 to 0.0464 across the three decoder families and $\times2/3/4$, while changes below $u=0.75$ are at most 0.0013. Matched RDN and smaller-MLP experiments replicate the high-frequency improvement and loss, respectively, with a second training seed. Decoder form and decoder capacity therefore have distinct empirical effects.

After retraining with Gaussian blur ($\sigma=0.6s$ HR pixels) and non-antialiased bicubic resizing, RDN improves PSNR at all scales but changes recovery below $u=0.75$ by at most 0.0006, failing the registered lower-band-gain prediction. This one-seed result extends low-frequency saturation to a second degradation. Table~\ref{tab:budget} and Fig.~\ref{fig:qual} show benchmark and visual effects.

\section{Cell responses persist as scale increases}
\label{sec:step}
\textbf{Equal multipliers imply unequal interventions.} For output side length $L$, the implementations multiply the supplied cell $2/L$ by the LR feature-grid size $L/s$. The decoder receives $c=2/s$ on each axis. Multiplying cell by $m$ therefore gives the per-axis step
\begin{equation}
h=\frac{2(m-1)}s,\qquad m=1+\frac{hs}{2}.
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
For fixed $m=2$, this step falls from 1 to $1/3$ between $\times2$ and $\times6$. A declining perturbation cost consequently combines changing input step with changing network response.

For native prediction $P$, perturbed prediction $P_h$, target $T$, native error $e=T-P$ and output change $\delta=P_h-P$, the MSE change is
\begin{equation}
D=\|e-\delta\|^2-\|e\|^2
=\underbrace{\|\delta\|^2}_{J}-2\operatorname{Re}\langle e,\delta\rangle.
\label{eq:exact}
\end{equation}
We report $J$, $D$ and the alignment term in RGB-mean MSE units. Small cost can arise from an output change that corrects existing error. The dB cost additionally depends on native MSE $V$ through $10\log_{10}(1+D/V)$.

\textbf{Sustained equal-step response.} At the same decoder-input step, cell-induced output changes generally do not weaken with scale and are often larger (Fig.~\ref{fig:step}). Across four datasets and three steps, $J_6/J_2\ge1$ in 11/12 conditions; point estimates span 0.94--2.47. The individual 95\% interval lower bounds span 0.81--1.57 and upper bounds 1.08--4.48; these ranges summarise 12 intervals, not a pooled interval. The smallest estimate is Urban100 at $h=0.25$, 0.94 $[0.81,1.08]$, and the largest is Set14 at $h=-0.25$, 2.47 $[1.47,4.48]$. This is a descriptive endpoint pattern, not a new preregistered success or a claim that every response increases significantly.

The frozen experiment measures all 246 full images from DIV2K 0869--0900, Set14, BSD100 and Urban100 with LIIF/LTE/SC-INR, two seeds and $h\in\{-0.25,0.25,0.5\}$, using paired within-dataset image bootstrap intervals. Matched image support, preprocessing, 10,000 resamples and multiplicity rules are documented in the repository; Table~\ref{tab:gates} separately reports the frozen existence, condition-wise and Set14 endpoint tests, including the failed Set14 prediction.

Response and cost can move in opposite directions. On BSD100 at $h=0.5$, $J_6/J_2=1.24$ $[1.06,1.44]$, but $D$ falls from $79.42$ to $40.77$ in $10^{-6}$ MSE (paired change $-38.65$ $[-55.13,-22.01]$; descriptive 95\% intervals, without multiplicity correction). Across datasets at this step, $r\ge0.375$ accounts for 43.5--50.7\% of $J$ at $\times2$, but only 2.4--3.0\% at $\times6$; the middle band $r\in[0.1875,0.375)$ accounts for 60.5--66.7\% at $\times6$.

These finite interventions compare responses across changing features and native conditions. Negative steps leave the training cell interval at some scales, and native $\times6$ is outside it; marginal support is reported for each condition.

\textbf{Scope of a scalar explanation.} A retrospective real-gain fit gives $Q=\sum_b(1-w_b)^2R_bE_b$, fitting $w_b$ to native and perturbed outputs. In the original DIV2K bands, doubled-cell $Q/D$ is 0.44, 0.72, 0.81 and 2.50 at $\times2/3/4/6$. Recovery and fitted-filter changes jointly account for 41.4\% of the $\times2\!\to\!\times6$ MSE-cost decline (Set14: 41.5\%); alignment and non-scalar terms complete the accounting. Projections, image rankings and negative cases remain in the repository.

\section{What cell contributes during learning}
\label{sec:training}
\textbf{Correct pairing supplies the training gain.} Native cell exceeds constant-cell and shuffled-cell training by 0.100\,dB $[0.061,0.149]$ and 0.097\,dB $[0.058,0.145]$, respectively (97.5\% paired-image intervals). Both seeds favour native cell in each comparison. Both controls also pass the registered no-cell equivalence tests: no-cell minus constant is $-0.005$\,dB $[-0.014,0.003]$, and no-cell minus shuffle is $-0.009$\,dB $[-0.017,-0.001]$ (95\% intervals), wholly within $\pm0.03$\,dB. Thus all four predictions pass (Table~\ref{tab:gates}). Retaining the extra inputs without correct scale pairing does not recover the native-cell benefit.

\textbf{Dimension-preserving controls.} Both new EDSR-LIIF arms retain the native decoder's two cell inputs and parameter count, under the same 200-epoch recipe and two seeds. The constant arm receives the training mean actual cell coordinate, $c_0=0.9242188453$, at every query and evaluation geometry; its input weights can be absorbed into the first-layer bias. The shuffled arm receives another sample's complete cell vector through a batch derangement, preserving the empirical marginal while removing correct pairing. Separate random generators preserve data and initialisation streams; assignment audits verify the interventions.

Frozen scoring uses epoch 200 regardless of validation and reevaluates native/no-cell baselines identically: full-image Y-PSNR on Set5/Set14/BSD100/Urban100 at four scales, plus DIV2K crop $R(u)$. Predictions require native-minus-control $\ge0.05$\,dB in the dataset- and scale-balanced mean, positive effects in both seeds and corrected intervals above zero; equivalence requires the entire 95\% interval inside $\pm0.03$\,dB. Image bootstrap intervals condition on the two seeds; full protocols and independent verification of all 50,328 records are in the repository.

\textbf{Frequency and inference-policy checks.} The recovery difference $R_{\rm native}-R_{\rm control}$ is small below $u=0.75$: its absolute quarter-band difference stays below 0.00048 for both controls at all four scales. At $\times2$, mean differences over $1\le u<2$ are 0.0146 $[0.0068,0.0245]$ for constant and 0.0165 $[0.0100,0.0246]$ for shuffle (descriptive 95\% intervals). Higher-scale region intervals often include zero, so these crop results do not imply a uniform bandwise advantage. For the shuffled model, switching among native, mean and four independent marginal-cell inference policies changes balanced benchmark PSNR by only 0.00014\,dB across all six policies. Its loss is therefore not recovered by these test-time cell assignments.

These controls support correctly paired explicit scale information as the source of the benefit in this recipe. They do not identify a unique internal mechanism: queries and features retain other scale cues, and shuffling also injects training noise. All original training runs completed; an authorised evaluation-only recovery resolved a CUDA-initialisation failure using unchanged weights and frozen evaluation/scoring code. The original failure and recovery provenance are retained.

\section{Conclusion and scope}
Recovery profiles are shared across tested decoders, while capacity gains concentrate above $u\approx0.75$. At equal decoder-input steps, cell response is sustained or larger in 11/12 endpoint comparisons. Shrinking steps and error alignment explain why a fixed-multiplier cost decline need not mean weaker output response. Correctly paired cell improves learning by 0.100/0.097\,dB over constant/shuffled controls, which are equivalent to no-cell within $\pm0.03$\,dB. The training gain depends on paired scale information, beyond the additional input dimensions.

Evidence is limited to the tested architectures, recipes and two degradations. New measurements were specified prospectively on previously studied images/checkpoints. Image-bootstrap intervals condition on fixed trained seeds; finite interventions and ablations do not identify a unique internal mechanism.
